# Stevo the Stalin

*An entirely fictional tale of the sternest sailor ever to patrol the snack cupboard.*

Stevo earned his nickname, **Stevo the Stalin**, on the first morning of the *SS Biscuit Tin*. It was not because he conquered anything. It was because he stood beside the galley with a clipboard and announced, “One biscuit per sailor. Two if you can explain what a biscuit is.”

The crew stared at him.

“Captain,” said First Mate Macca, “we all know what biscuits are.”

“Excellent,” Stevo replied, making a note. “That saves time.”

Stevo ran a tight ship. His socks were folded into precise little squares. His compass was polished so brightly that once it pointed north and blinded a seagull. He even made the crew line up for morning tea by height, which was difficult because one sailor was a parrot and another was a very tall mop.

Then, on the third day at sea, a fog rolled in so thick that the ship’s bell sounded like it was ringing from inside a pudding. The *SS Biscuit Tin* drifted off course.

Stevo studied the map. Macca studied the map upside down. The parrot studied a cracker.

“Captain,” Macca said, “we could ask the lighthouse.”

“A lighthouse cannot answer questions,” said Stevo.

Right then, a voice floated through the fog: “You’re heading toward the sandbar!”

It was the parrot, who had memorised the lighthouse keeper’s warning from the day before. Stevo immediately ordered a turn. The ship swung around just in time. A moment later, the sandbar appeared beside them, wearing a tiny wave like a hat.

The crew cheered. Stevo looked at his clipboard.

“Emergency biscuit ration,” he declared. “One each.”

Macca raised an eyebrow. “What about the parrot? He saved the ship.”

Stevo considered this carefully, then handed the parrot a biscuit.

The parrot ate it in one bite and squawked, “Another!”

“Absolutely not,” said Stevo. “But you may have the crumbs.”

That evening, Stevo loosened the rules. The crew could take two biscuits if they shared one, and the mop was promoted to Assistant Lighthouse, mostly because it was tall enough to see over the fog.

From then on, the sailors still called him Stevo the Stalin—but only when he was counting biscuits. When he shared them, they called him Captain Stevo.

And the parrot? It became ship’s navigator, snack inspector, and the only sailor allowed to eat the evidence.
